    Recorder and storage

    Headroom covers file system space, bitrate swings at night and drives that should not run full. 10 to 20% is typical. The incoming limit is on the recorder's spec sheet, often listed as incoming or input bandwidth.

    8. NVR incoming bandwidth is every camera's full bitrate added up. Motion-only recording saves disk space, but the cameras still stream to the recorder all day.

    These are rounded middle-of-the-road figures from published bitrate guides and manufacturer recommendations, which vary by a factor of two or more for the same camera class. Real bitrates depend on the scene, lighting, night IR noise (often the busiest time for the encoder), lens and camera settings such as quality, keyframe interval and bitrate cap. Smart codec savings vary the most and shrink in busy scenes. Use the camera's own bitrate when you know it, and check the recorder after a few days of real recording.
